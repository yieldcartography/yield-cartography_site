% 2 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \kicker{Round numbers as risk governance}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Bank-internal governance frameworks escalate when a forecasting
          error, of a balance-sheet item, of net interest income, of a
          stress-test projection, exceeds a threshold. In practice the
          threshold is a round number, commonly three percent, imposed
          uniformly across institutions and portfolios.
    \item A fixed threshold has no distributional content: whether a 3\%
          error is routine noise or a genuine signal depends entirely on the
          institution's own forecasting error distribution.
    \item The paper proposes and evaluates calibrating escalation limits to
          that distribution.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 3 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The calibration rule}
  \kicker{Thresholds as quantiles}
  \small For institution $i$ with empirical forecasting-error distribution
  $F_i$ of the monitored quantity, the escalation limit at tolerance
  $\alpha$ is the quantile
  \[ c_{i,\alpha} \;=\; F_{|e|,i}^{-1}\!\left(1-\alpha\right),
     \qquad \text{so that} \qquad
     \Pr\!\left(|e_{it}| > c_{i,\alpha}\right) \;=\; \alpha . \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Under the quantile rule, the escalation frequency is equalised
          across institutions by construction; under a uniform fixed
          threshold it is whatever the institution's error dispersion makes
          it.
    \item The rule requires only the institution's own forecasting track
          record, an input every supervised bank already possesses.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 4 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Evidence}
  \kicker{Polish bank stress-test data}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The calibration is developed on Polish bank stress-test forecast
          data across institutions of different size and macroeconomic
          exposure.
    \item A uniform 3\% threshold either grossly under-triggers or
          over-triggers depending on the institution: for a large,
          diversified balance sheet it fires on routine noise, for a small
          or macro-exposed institution it misses genuinely informative
          errors.
    \item The quantile-calibrated limits restore a common escalation
          probability and make threshold breaches comparable across the
          system.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 5 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Implications}
  \kicker{For risk governance and supervision}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Escalation frameworks gain statistical content: a breach carries a
          known and uniform surprise probability, so it is informative by
          design.
    \item Supervisory comparability improves, since breach counts across
          institutions measure the same tail event.
    \item The rule composes with the existing governance apparatus; it
          replaces only the number, an institution-specific quantile in place
          of a system-wide constant.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
