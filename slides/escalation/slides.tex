\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \kicker{NII forecasting and IRRBB governance}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Forecast accuracy for net interest income (NII) and interest-rate
          risk in the banking book (IRRBB) is central to earnings stability,
          balance-sheet management and supervisory credibility.
    \item Many institutions govern forecast performance with fixed deviation
          thresholds (3/4/5\%), even though forecast uncertainty widens with
          the horizon and the error process may be heavy-tailed. A flat limit
          therefore carries no consistent probabilistic interpretation.
    \item The paper develops an integrated, probability-coherent framework
          for monitoring NII forecast errors and assessing IRRBB limit
          breaches.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 3 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Horizon-specific, quantile-anchored thresholds}
  \kicker{The SEP fan-chart logic applied to escalation limits}
  \small Following the Federal Reserve's RMSE-based communication of forecast
  uncertainty, thresholds are anchored to the horizon-specific error scale:
  \[ c_{h,q} \;=\; z_q \cdot RMSE(h),
     \qquad z_{80}=0.84,\; z_{90}=1.28,\; z_{95}=1.64,\; z_{99}=2.33 \]
  under the Normal/RMSE mapping used for communication, with empirical
  quantiles replacing $z_q\,RMSE(h)$ where the error history warrants it.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A standardized exceedance score expresses each realized error as a
          proportion of the 95\% boundary, giving dashboards one common unit
          across horizons.
    \item Validation: unconditional and conditional coverage tests on the
          resulting interval forecasts, quantile elicitability, and a
          bias-dispersion decomposition separating structural misspecification
          from random variation.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 4 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The outer tier: extreme value theory}
  \kicker{POT-GPD calibration of rare misses}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Because NII forecast errors may be heavy-tailed, the outer
          escalation tier is calibrated by peaks-over-threshold: by the
          Pickands--Balkema--de Haan theorem, excesses over a high threshold
          converge to a Generalized Pareto Distribution.
    \item POT-GPD modelling stabilises the outer tier against rare but severe
          misses, in analogy to Expected-Shortfall extensions of the VaR
          traffic-light backtest.
    \item The IRRBB extension quantifies the probability that limits on
          $\Delta$NII are breached solely due to forecast or model
          uncertainty, mapping model risk into risk-appetite language.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 5 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Empirical illustration and implications}
  \kicker{Simulation, N=120 per horizon at 3m/6m/12m/24m}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item A transparent simulation reports empirical 90\%/95\% thresholds and
          EVT-based 97.5\% outer-tier limits per horizon, demonstrates their
          stability, and contrasts them with flat limits, which over-penalise
          long-horizon errors and under-weight short-horizon ones.
    \item Comparability is restored across horizons because dispersion growth
          is embedded in the limit definition; conditional-coverage testing
          detects clustering of exceptions; breach probabilities for
          $\Delta$NII are suitable for ALCO reporting.
    \item Limits of the approach: calibration is data-hungry at long
          horizons, and regime or model changes can break stationarity,
          requiring re-baselining, shadow backtests and, at adoption, wider
          bands or pooled-horizon estimation.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
