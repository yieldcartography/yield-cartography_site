\begin{frame}{Motivation}
  \kicker{XVA needs a joint, simulable curve model}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Valuation adjustments (CVA, DVA, FVA and related XVA terms) on
          interest-rate derivative portfolios require simulating the entire
          curve system, discounting and projection curves jointly, over long
          horizons and across collateralised and uncollateralised netting
          sets.
    \item The Heath--Jarrow--Morton framework prices consistently by
          construction, but a generic HJM volatility makes the forward-curve
          state non-Markovian, which is computationally prohibitive in an
          XVA engine.
    \item The paper constructs a parametrisation that is simultaneously
          Markovian and consistent with the post-crisis multi-curve setting.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 3 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{The HJM framework and the Cheyette subclass}
  \kicker{Model class}
  \small Under the risk-neutral measure the instantaneous forward curve
  follows
  \[ df(t,T) \;=\; \alpha(t,T)\,dt + \sigma(t,T)\,dW_t,
     \qquad
     \alpha(t,T) \;=\; \sigma(t,T)\!\int_t^{T}\!\sigma(t,s)\,ds, \]
  with the drift pinned down by no-arbitrage.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The paper demonstrates the importance of the Cheyette subclass,
          separable volatility structures that collapse the curve dynamics
          onto a finite-dimensional Markovian state, and derives the
          instantaneous forward-rate dynamics of that form generically.
    \item Markovianity is what makes the specification implementable inside
          an XVA engine, where the whole curve system is simulated over long
          horizons.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 4 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Multi-curve toolkit}
  \kicker{Post-crisis market structure}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item After the financial crisis, IBOR-based deposit rates and OIS rates
          diverged abruptly, from a plateau of a few basis points to more
          than 200 bp at the peak, and FRA par rates detached from the
          corresponding forwards: discounting and projection require separate
          curves.
    \item The paper develops the multi-curve construction and its XVA
          integration, with pricing formulae for the vanilla non-linear
          building blocks (swaptions, caplets and floorlets) in the
          multi-curve setting.
    \item The focus is a relatively less liquid market, the Polish PLN curve
          system, and the calibration problems that illiquidity creates.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% 5 ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Calibration results and implications}
  \kicker{One-factor Cheyette models on PLN data}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Data availability disciplines the model choice: with swaption
          volatilities only, and no active caps and floors market, a
          three-factor exponential specification cannot be calibrated on the
          PLN market.
    \item Calibrations of several one-factor Cheyette-class models show that
          a relatively simple specification, Hull--White with two summands,
          achieves satisfactory calibration quality and computation time.
    \item Practical guidance for the XVA engine: the calibration step can be
          scheduled ahead of scenario generation, and the XVA integral should
          be discretised no finer than the simulation's horizon steps;
          overall performance depends on many degrees of freedom beyond the
          model choice (time-scale granularity, interpolation, minimisation
          method).
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}
